Assessing Household Food Security and Dietary Diversity in Nigeria
Evidence from the FAO DIEM Round 3 Survey (2022)
Author: Akinwonjowo Olayinka
Project Overview
This project assesses household food security and dietary diversity in
five selected northern Nigerian states---Adamawa, Borno, Katsina, Yobe,
and Zamfara---using secondary data from the Food and Agriculture
Organization (FAO) Data in Emergencies (DIEM) Monitoring System,
Household Survey Round 3 (2022).

The analysis examines food insecurity, household dietary diversity,
differences by state and gender of household head, livelihood coping
strategies, and exposure to major shocks.

The final cleaned analytical sample comprised 1,398 households.
Research Objectives
The study was designed to:
- Determine the prevalence of household food insecurity.
- Assess household dietary diversity levels.
- Examine differences in food insecurity by state and gender of
  household head.
- Analyse livelihood coping strategies and major shocks among
  food-insecure households.
Dataset and Indicators
- Data source: FAO Data in Emergencies (DIEM) Monitoring System --
  Nigeria, Round 3 (2022).
- Geographical coverage: Adamawa, Borno, Katsina, Yobe, and
  Zamfara States.
- Final sample: 1,398 households after data cleaning.
Key indicators analysed:
- Food insecurity status, based on the Food Insecurity Experience
  Scale (FIES).
- Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS).
- Livelihood Coping Strategies Index (LCSI).
- Exposure to shocks, including violence/insecurity and flooding.

Key Findings
1. Overall Food Insecurity
- 39.3% of households (550 out of 1,398) were food insecure.
2. Food Insecurity by State
  State       Food-insecurity prevalence
  Borno                            46.4%
Zamfara                          39.6%
Yobe                             38.2%
Katsina                          37.7%
Adamawa                          34.9%

Borno recorded the highest food-insecurity prevalence, while Adamawa
recorded the lowest among the five states analysed.
3. Food Insecurity by Gender of Household Head
  Household head               Food-insecurity prevalence
  Female-headed households                          51.1%
Male-headed households                            38.5%

Female-headed households had a higher reported prevalence of food
insecurity than male-headed households.
4. Household Dietary Diversity
  Household category           Average HDDS
  All households                       5.77
Food-secure households                6.4
Food-insecure households              4.8

Food-insecure households recorded a lower average Household Dietary
Diversity Score than food-secure households.
5. Livelihood Coping Strategies Among Food-Insecure Households
  Coping strategy category     Percentage
  No coping                          6.9%
Stress strategies                 46.2%
Crisis strategies                 17.8%
Emergency strategies              29.1%

The findings show that many food-insecure households were using
livelihood coping strategies, including crisis and emergency strategies.
6. Major Shocks and Food Insecurity
  Shock exposure          Food-insecurity prevalence
  Violence/insecurity                          66.4%
Flooding                                     62.7%

Food-insecurity prevalence was high among households reporting exposure
to violence/insecurity and flooding.
Discussion
The findings show geographic and gender differences in household food
insecurity across the selected states. Borno recorded the highest
prevalence, while female-headed households were more affected than
male-headed households.

The difference in average dietary diversity between food-secure and
food-insecure households highlights the importance of considering both
food access and dietary variety. The reported use of crisis and
emergency livelihood coping strategies also points to vulnerability
among food-insecure households.

Households exposed to violence/insecurity and flooding recorded high
food-insecurity prevalence, highlighting the importance of considering
conflict and climate-related shocks in food-security analysis.
Conclusion
The study demonstrates that household food insecurity and lower dietary
diversity were important challenges among the surveyed households in the
five selected northern Nigerian states. Borno, female-headed households,
and households exposed to major shocks were among the groups with higher
reported food-insecurity prevalence.
Recommendations
Based on the study findings, the report recommends:
1. Prioritising food and livelihood assistance in Borno and other
   high-burden states.
2. Designing targeted interventions for female-headed households.
3. Promoting dietary diversity within food-security and agricultural
   programmes.
4. Strengthening early-warning and rapid-response systems for conflict
   and flood shocks.
5. Supporting households to move away from emergency coping strategies
   through sustainable livelihood opportunities.
6. Encouraging regular use of DIEM and similar data for evidence-based
   decision-making.

Project Scope and Data Note
This is a secondary-data analysis of the FAO DIEM Nigeria Round 3 (2022)
household survey. The findings describe the 1,398-household analytical
sample across the five selected states; they should not automatically be
interpreted as estimates for every household in Nigeria.

The dataset year is 2022. The results should therefore be understood in
the context of the survey period and the selected study areas.
